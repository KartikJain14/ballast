## 1. Shape and continuity

- **8,760 rows**, hourly, `2026-01-01 00:00:00` → `2026-12-31 23:00:00`. 2026
  is not a leap year, so 365×24 = 8,760 is exactly right.
- **0 duplicate timestamps, 0 missing hours** — the index is perfectly
  continuous. No DST artifact (a DST spring-forward would show as a missing
  hour in March; a fall-back as a duplicate in November — neither appears).
  **Conclusion:** timestamps are naive wall-clock hours with no DST
  adjustment. Encoded as `DataAssumptions.timezone_note` in `engine/config.py`.
- All 16 expected columns present and parseable; booleans for
  `repo_sweep_window_open` / `repo_sweep_mature` parse cleanly.

## 2. Sign convention and the net-flow identity

`net_flow_usd == deposits_usd - withdrawals_usd` holds to **$0.01** across all
8,760 rows (floating-point rounding only — effectively exact). This resolves
Trap #2 from the brief: **positive = inflow**, confirmed.

- Mean deposits/hour: **$10,141,915**
- Mean withdrawals/hour: **$10,430,808**
- Gross throughput: ≈ **$20.57M/hour**, which matches the buildathon
  statement's "$20,000,000 in deposits, withdrawals, and settlements every
  hour" almost exactly — confirming that figure is **gross**, not net (Trap:
  "gross vs. net flows").
- Mean net flow: **–$288,893/hour** → the book is very slightly net-outflow
  over the simulated year (≈ –1.4% of deposits annualized against the
  deposit base). **51.4%** of hours are net-outflow hours.

## 3. Units and ranges of every rate/haircut column

| Column | Min | Max | Mean | Unique values |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `tier1_repo_rate_apy` | 3.725 | 4.374 | 4.055 | continuous (floating) |
| `tier2_bond_yield_pct` | 3.760 | 5.924 | 4.748 | continuous (floating) |
| `tier3_stock_volatility_pct` | 18.0 | 18.0 | 18.0 | **1 (constant)** |
| `tier1_early_redemption_haircut_pct` | 0.5 | 0.5 | 0.5 | **1 (constant)** |
| `tier2_bond_liquidation_haircut_pct` | 2.0 | 2.0 | 2.0 | **1 (constant)** |
| `tier3_stock_liquidation_haircut_pct` | 15.0 | 15.0 | 15.0 | **1 (constant)** |
| `emergency_overdraft_penalty_apr_pct` | 12.0 | 12.0 | 12.0 | **1 (constant)** |

**Finding that revises the brief:** the brief states "haircut and penalty
columns are time series, not constants — always read the row value." In
*this* generated dataset, all four haircut/penalty columns are **constant for
the entire year** — only the two yield columns (`tier1_repo_rate_apy`,
`tier2_bond_yield_pct`) actually float. Per Operating Rule #2 ("if the data
contradicts this brief, the data wins"), Ballast still reads these columns
**per-row** everywhere (the code makes no assumption of constancy — see
`engine/simulator/tiers.py`, which takes the rate/haircut as a function
argument every time), so the engine would handle a time-varying haircut
correctly if one were ever present. We simply report, honestly, that in this
dataset's year there is nothing to pick up by doing so.

All percent columns read naturally as percentage *points* (0.5 = 0.5%, 12.0 =
12% APR), consistent with the brief's own worked numbers (0.5% break fee,
etc.) — no unit ambiguity once cross-checked against the brief's constants.

## 6. Spike catalogue (top 1% hours by |net flow|)

Threshold for the top 1%: **|net flow| ≥ $13.48M** (88 hours, ≈1% of 8,760 as
expected). Most positive spikes cluster almost entirely on the **1st and

Most negative spikes include recognizable U.S. calendar events but not

- **2026-11-28 15:00 (–$27.1M)** — Saturday of Thanksgiving/Black Friday
  weekend (Thanksgiving 2026 = Nov 26, Black Friday = Nov 27).
- **2026-07-04 09:00 (–$25.4M)** — Independence Day.
- Several others (2026-11-14, 11-13, 10-02, 06-28, 08-06, 08-29, 10-10) have
  **no obvious calendar explanation** — we report this honestly rather than
  force a holiday narrative onto noise the generator produced. A production
  system should treat the holiday/shopping-event flags as *hypotheses to
  test* via backtest lift, not facts to hardcode.

## 8. Change-point detection

**Methodology note (a real trap we hit and fixed):** we first tried
`ruptures.Pelt(model="rbf")` with a penalty search, per the brief's
suggestion. It **hung indefinitely** — `predict()` never returned within
100s even on this modest 8,760-point series, because the chosen penalty
scale caused pathological candidate-set growth under the rbf cost model. We
switched to `ruptures.Binseg(model="l2")` with a **fixed `n_bkps`**, which is
O(n log n) and has bounded, predictable runtime (completes in <0.5s). This is
recorded in `docs/DECISIONS.md`.

- **Net flow breakpoints** (6 requested): all six detected breakpoints fall
  in **December** (Dec 11, 14, 16, 21, 25, 27) — a late-year regime shift in
  flow statistics around the holiday season.
- **Repo rate breakpoints**: spread through the year (Mar 12, Mar 25, Jun 9,
  Jun 24, Oct 22, Dec 23) — consistent with a slowly, continuously floating
  policy-linked rate rather than discrete rate-decision jumps.

## 9. Residual autocorrelation (ACF/PACF, Ljung-Box)

After removing hour-of-day seasonality, Ljung-Box at lags 24 and 168 both
reject i.i.d. residuals overwhelmingly (**p ≈ 0.0**, statistics 14,456 and
26,211). After removing the richer hour-of-day × day-of-week seasonality,
rejection is still overwhelming (statistics 6,599 and 9,335, p ≈ 0.0).
**Conclusion:** simple seasonal-mean removal does not whiten the series —
there is real additional structure (see Section 11, the weekend effect) that
a forecaster must capture with lag/rolling features, not just calendar
dummies. See `reports/figures/acf_pacf_residuals.png`.

## 10. Flow response to market factors

| Pair | Correlation (lag 0) |
|---|---|
| net flow vs. equity return | **–0.007** |
| net flow vs. repo-rate change | **+0.010** |
| withdrawals vs. equity return | **–0.0002** |

All statistically negligible. **The synthetic generator does not couple

rate-sensitivity effect in the forecaster, because the data contains none —
claiming otherwise would be exactly the kind of fabricated sophistication
Operating Rule #1 forbids.

## 11. The overnight path-max `M` — the central risk variable, and its most important structural feature

Computed over all 364 complete 17:00→08:00 windows in the year:

| Statistic | Value |
|---|---|
| Mean | $10,854,474 |
| Std | $9,865,792 |
| P50 | $6,111,864 |
| P90 | $26,791,470 |
| P95 | $28,606,312 |
| P99 | $31,909,714 |
| Max | $38,114,281 |
| Min | –$2,384,039 |

The headline finding is **not** the summary stats — it's the shape
(`reports/figures/overnight_pathmax_hist.png`): **`M` is sharply bimodal.**
Splitting by the day-of-week the 17:00 sweep starts explains it completely
(`reports/figures/overnight_pathmax_by_dow.png`):

| Night starting | Mean M | Median M |
|---|---|---|
| Mon | $5.02M | $4.44M |
| Tue | $4.93M | $5.13M |
| Wed | $4.46M | $4.36M |
| Thu | $6.69M | $6.27M |
| **Fri** | **$25.69M** | **$25.92M** |
| **Sat** | **$25.40M** | **$25.26M** |
| Sun | $3.80M | $3.08M |

**Friday-night and Saturday-night overnight windows carry ~5x the path-max
outflow risk of any other night of the week.** This is not a payday effect
(we checked — day-of-month is uniformly spread across the high-risk nights);
it is a pure weekend-spending effect baked into the generator. It is also
*not* about weekend repo-carry mechanics (that affects accrual duration, not
customer flow risk) — it is genuinely a customer-behavior seasonality.

**This is a first-order design decision, not a footnote.** A single pooled
buffer sized off `F_M` for all nights would be forced to choose between
chronically over-buffering (holding Friday/Saturday-sized cash Sun–Thu, i.e.
≈$20M/night × 5 nights × ~250+ nights/year of needlessly foregone interest)
or under-covering weekend nights. **Ballast's newsvendor/LP sizing is
conditioned on day-of-week (specifically a `is_fri_or_sat_night` feature)**,
not a single pooled distribution — see `docs/METHODOLOGY.md`. This is one of
the clearest "verify against the data, don't trust the brief's framing"
moments in the whole build (Operating Rule #2).

**Tail validity caveat (Trap: "tails unverifiable above ~99%"):** with 364
nights, the expected number of exceedances of a *true* 99.9th percentile is
**0.364** — stated explicitly in the brief's Section 3.10 as the benchmark,
and matched almost exactly here. Any buffer quantile above ~99% is
necessarily **model-based (EVT/parametric extrapolation), not empirically
observable** in-sample. We say this on-screen in the dashboard everywhere a
q>99% buffer is shown, and we always condition tail estimates on the
day-of-week regime above, since a single EVT fit, in particular, would again
be contaminated by mixing two very different severity sub-populations.

See also the QQ plot (`overnight_pathmax_qq.png`, strongly non-normal, exactly
as expected from the bimodal mixture) and the mean-excess plot
(`mean_excess_plot.png`) used to sanity-check an EVT threshold.

## 12. Bond index convention: price-only, confirmed

Regressing realized `tier2_bond_price_index` returns against the
duration-approximation `-D_mod·Δy`:

- Correlation: **0.9998**
- Mean residual: **1.17e-7** (effectively zero)
- Residual std: **9.84e-6** (tiny)

A price-only index's returns should be *fully* explained by
`-D_mod·Δy` with a zero-mean residual; a total-return index would show a
small but persistent **positive** mean residual (coupon drift not captured
by the duration term). We observe the former.
**Conclusion: `tier2_bond_price_index` is a price-only index.**
`BondIndexConvention.PRICE_ONLY` in `engine/config.py` is confirmed, not just
assumed — carry/yield accrual must be added on top of index price changes
when valuing a bond position, and that is exactly what
`engine/simulator/tiers.py::bond_carry` plus `bond_price_return` do
separately (no double-counting).

## 13. Repo rate dynamics and equity volatility

- Repo rate moves in small continuous steps: mean |Δ| = 0.004 pct-points/hr,
  max |Δ| = 0.018 pct-points/hr, unchanged in only 7.7% of hours — a smoothly
  floating short rate, not a step-function policy rate.
- `tier3_stock_volatility_pct` is **literally constant at 18.0%** all year —
  it is a fixed input *parameter* to the price-generation process, not a
  realized/estimated volatility series. We do not attempt to "forecast" it;
  we treat it as a given constant in the equity-sleeve risk/EV calculations.

## 14. Opening balance — unparameterized in the data

The dataset contains flows, not a balance sheet; there is no "true" opening
cash balance. We parameterize `opening_cash_usd` and `deposit_base_usd`
(default: **$20,000,000** each, matching the brief's framing) in
`engine/config.py::DataAssumptions`, and report every dollar KPI **also** in
basis points and $-per-$100M so headline numbers are legible regardless of
the chosen scale.
